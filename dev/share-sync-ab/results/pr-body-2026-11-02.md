## What changed

- **Sender reports.** A stream with nothing written reported RTP time zero against the real clock, and libwebrtc keeps twenty reports. The engine then worked from false points for up to half a minute, and sound added to a picture-only share ran up to 900 ms late. `StreamTx` now leaves the report out until the first packet and keeps the SDES, so RTCP's cadence is unchanged.
- `screen.audio.sync` returns one audio and one video RTP time of the same instant for the viewer's slot. Only the SFU can say this, because it rewrites RTP times per viewer.
- Once a second the viewer compares where each side comes out. The desktop mixer reports when a sample reaches the speaker (`share_playback_clock`).
- **Sync aims 40 ms late**, the middle of the −45…+125 ms range.
- **The picture waits**, through `jitterBufferTarget`, after ten looks and only when sound is 45 ms past the aim. Earlier or smaller waits made a start and a jittery link worse than the engine alone.
- **In the desktop app sound can wait too**, when the picture is late with nothing left to give. The mixer plays 1 % slower, 10 ms a second, at most 400 ms.
- **In a browser, sound is left to the engine's own sync.**
- **After a stall** a viewer drops the late backlog before its receiver sees it (`late-frames.ts`, `RTCRtpScriptTransform`), keeping keyframes, and `screen.stalled` asks for a keyframe and restores the estimate. The filter acts only after arrivals stopped for 200 ms, so a plain queue is left alone.
- **A new watch is silent until its picture is on screen**, for 1.5 s at most. The first frame is not touched.
- Voice never waits. A share without sound never enters this. Muting or deafening releases the waits, also while paused. An older server means no lip-sync, and a lost query is asked again.
